# Video prompt examples / 视频提示词示例

These examples demonstrate writing only. Choose views, dialogue and segment lengths to fit the current brief; split a long cooking sequence into separate clips before using it, carrying the same identity and continuing states into each prompt. The examples assume vertical footage with no requested music or subtitles; follow explicit aspect-ratio, music and subtitle requests independently.
以下示例只示范写法。按当前需求与片段时长安排画面和台词；较长做菜过程须先拆成独立片段，每段沿用同一身份和持续状态。示例假设竖屏且未要求配乐或字幕；实际分别服从用户的画幅、配乐和字幕要求。

## Continuous selfie: entering a market / 连续自拍：进菜场

```text
Raw vertical smartphone footage. The creator films herself on her phone's front camera, holding it at arm's length just above eye level as she walks into a busy indoor neighborhood market in the morning; the frame is exactly what her phone sees, and the phone itself never appears. Retain the creator's identity from the supplied reference; if none is supplied, use a woman in her late twenties with short black hair, a red knit sweater and a light Shanghai accent, and keep these traits across related clips. Warm bulbs hang over rows of stalls, handwritten red price tags stick out of piles of vegetables, and older shoppers squeeze past with cloth bags. The picture bobs gently with her steps; when someone brushes her shoulder she lifts the phone a little to bring her face back to the center. She glances around, slightly unsure, then looks into the lens and says (on camera, Mandarin, soft clear voice with a light Shanghai accent): "以前都是外婆买好的。" She takes a breath, looks ahead toward the stalls, and adds as if cheering herself on: "今天，自己来。" Sound: overlapping market chatter, a vendor calling out prices in the distance, plastic bags rustling, her synchronous voice close to the phone mic. No background music, no subtitles; retain the market's scene text.
```

## Supported wide views and friend-held close-ups: tomato and eggs / 固定机位加朋友手持特写：做番茄炒蛋

```text
Raw vertical smartphone footage of a home cooking vlog in a small Shanghai apartment kitchen, lit by late-afternoon window light: white laminate counter, a wooden chopping board, a small two-burner stove. Keep the same creator identity, red knit sleeves pushed up and the same light Shanghai accent across these clips; use the supplied identity reference or the established no-reference description. The phone's rear camera rests in a stable stand at chest height for the wider views, and a friend lifts it for close-ups; the creator keeps both hands free for cooking. She stays mostly at the edge of the frame, looking at the food rather than the lens.
From the supported phone, the picture holds still as she steadies two ripe tomatoes with one hand and cuts them into uneven chunks with the other; juice pools on the board and the knife taps the wood in a quick rhythm.
Cut to a friend-held close-up from slightly above so viewers can see the eggs and bowl: she cracks an egg on the rim of a white bowl, a small piece of shell falls in, and she retrieves it with a chopstick. She steadies the bowl with one hand and beats the eggs with the other. The friend makes a small framing correction to keep the bowl visible.
Cut to a friend-held oblique view from safely beside the stove so viewers can see the egg set in the wok: she pours in the beaten eggs, sets down the bowl, and uses a spatula while her other hand steadies the wok handle. She transfers the still-glossy eggs to a bowl beside the stove. Added voiceover accompanies this stage: "鸡蛋先炒到七分熟盛出来，"
Cut back to the phone in its stand to show the remaining ingredients and her hands together: the tomato chunks go into the wok and hiss. She stirs them into a red sauce, returns the eggs, sets down the bowl and sprinkles a small spoon of sugar. The added voiceover continues with the corresponding actions: "番茄炒出汁，再倒回去。外婆讲的，上海人的番茄炒蛋，要放一点点糖。" After returning the eggs, her red sleeves remain pushed up; no new clothing or person appears between clips.
The added voiceover is Mandarin, soft and clear with the same light Shanghai accent, relaxed like telling a friend. It sounds like a separate quiet-room recording, with an even level rather than the changing distance of the cooking camera. She does not lip-sync to it; when her face appears, she can breathe, smile and react naturally to the cooking. Synchronous phone-mic sound remains tied to the camera position: knife on wood, an egg cracking, oil sizzling, the spatula scraping the wok and a range hood humming. No background music, no subtitles.
```
